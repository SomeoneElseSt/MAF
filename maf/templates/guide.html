User Guide

Making a new post

Click Submit at the top of any page. Give your post a title, then add a link, some text, or both. Posts can't be edited once they're up, so read it over first.

If your post looks like it names someone, you'll be asked to check before it goes up. You can edit it, or post anyway if it's a public figure.

What is required and not

  • A title is required.
  • You need a link or some text. Either one is enough.
  • Hashtags are optional.

Commenting under a post

Write in the box under the post and click Comment. To answer a specific comment, click Reply under it. Under the box you'll see the name you're using in this thread.

You can also reply privately. Tick "Make only visible to …" under the box and only you and that person will see the reply, inside the thread. Everyone else sees the thread as if it weren't there. Replies inside a private exchange stay private. Private replies belong to the device you posted from, so you'll only see them there.

Reacting to a post or comment

Click the small smiley under a post or comment and pick an emoji. Click a reaction that's already there to add yours, and click it again to take it back. You can use each emoji once per post or comment.

Hashtags

Add up to 3 hashtags when you make a post, separated by commas, like #berlin, #help. You can make up new ones. Click a hashtag, or the # in the search bar, to see every post with it.

Four hashtags come with a suggested outline for your post: #help, #wondering, #confession and #hottakes. #hottakes is for unpopular opinions. Those posts are highlighted, and replies should argue with the take, not the person.

Names change per thread

In each thread you get a random name like quiet-otter, so a conversation can be followed. In the next thread you get a different one. There are no profiles and no post history.

The rule

Don't name students or staff. Public figures are fine.

Flags

Five flags take a post off the front page and out of search. It stays reachable by its link, where anyone can ask for it to come back or be removed for good.

What can be seen

  • The university can see that a code email reached your inbox, since it runs that inbox.
  • The server sees posts and names. It doesn't see emails and doesn't log IP addresses.
  • The timing of posts and shared networks can still give clues, and you're trusting that the server runs the published source code.

New device

Sign in again with your email. There's no password.

Using the data

You can read MAF from your own tools. Use this data explains how.
